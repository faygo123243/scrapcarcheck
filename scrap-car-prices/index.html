What Affects the Scrap Price of a Car?

Steel price. The biggest single factor. UK scrap dealers buy cars primarily as steel. When global steel demand rises, so does the price. In 2022, prices spiked to over £200 per tonne; they have since settled back. You can track live steel prices at the London Metal Exchange.

Vehicle weight. Heavier cars are worth more for scrap. A large SUV contains far more steel than a city car. When getting a quote, dealers will either weigh your car or use the manufacturer's kerb weight to calculate an offer.

Condition. A complete car with all its main components (engine, gearbox, catalytic converter, wheels, battery) will get a better price than one that has been stripped. Missing a catalytic converter in particular can reduce the value significantly — cats contain platinum, palladium and rhodium and are worth hundreds of pounds on their own.

Non-ferrous metals. Catalytic converters, aluminium alloy wheels, copper wiring and the battery (especially on electric vehicles) can add significantly to the scrap value of a car over and above the steel alone.

Location. Scrap prices can vary by 10–15% across different parts of the UK. Areas with many competing ATFs tend to offer better prices. Using a comparison service like Scrap Car Check ensures you get the best local rate.

How to Get the Best Scrap Price

Compare multiple dealers. The easiest way to get the best price is to use a comparison service — the spread between the highest and lowest quote is often £50–£100, which is pure money left on the table if you only call one dealer.

Keep your catalytic converter. Do not let anyone remove your catalytic converter before collection. The full-car price will be higher than the scrap metal price for the cat alone.

Check if any parts have individual sale value. Before scrapping, check whether any individual parts — alloy wheels, infotainment systems, seats — are worth selling separately on eBay or Facebook Marketplace. This takes more effort but can add to the total return.
