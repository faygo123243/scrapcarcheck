Glasgow and the surrounding central belt including East Kilbride, Paisley, Motherwell, Clydebank and Hamilton have a strong network of Authorised Treatment Facilities (ATFs) registered with SEPA, Scotland's environmental regulator. Unlike in England and Wales where the Environment Agency oversees ATFs, Scottish scrap dealers must meet the requirements set by the Scottish Environment Protection Agency to operate legally and issue a Certificate of Destruction. The scrap market in central Scotland is competitive and prices track UK steel prices closely, meaning Glasgow residents can expect prices broadly in line with the national average.

Are ATFs in Scotland regulated the same way as in England?

No. In England and Wales, Authorised Treatment Facilities are regulated and licensed by the Environment Agency. In Scotland, the equivalent regulator is SEPA, the Scottish Environment Protection Agency. The end result for you as a vehicle owner is the same: a SEPA registered ATF in Scotland has the same legal authority to issue a Certificate of Destruction as an Environment Agency licensed ATF in England. The Certificate is recognised by the DVLA across the whole of the UK.
