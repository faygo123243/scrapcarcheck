Scrap Car FAQs

Everything you need to know about scrapping your car in the UK.

Getting a Price

Most scrap cars in the UK are worth between £100 and £500, though this varies widely depending on the weight, make, model and current scrap metal prices. Heavier vehicles such as SUVs and vans tend to fetch more because scrap prices are calculated per tonne. Enter your registration on ScrapCarCheck to get live quotes from dealers in your area.

Scrap car prices are based primarily on the weight of your vehicle multiplied by the current scrap steel price per tonne. Non-ferrous metals such as aluminium and copper also contribute to the value. The condition of the car and whether key parts such as the catalytic converter are still present will affect the final offer.

The main factors are the vehicle's kerb weight, the live price of scrap steel, whether the catalytic converter is intact, the fuel type and whether the engine is complete. Petrol and diesel cars are valued differently from electric vehicles, which contain valuable battery materials but can be more expensive to process.

A part exchange with a dealer will sometimes yield a higher nominal figure, but that value is applied as a discount off a new car rather than cash in hand. If your car is at the end of its life or in a condition a dealer would not want, scrapping it will almost always put more money in your pocket.

Selling parts privately before scrapping can increase your total returns, but it takes time and a scrap dealer may reduce their offer if key components are missing. Catalytic converters, alloy wheels and intact engines are the parts most likely to add real value when sold separately. If you remove any parts, tell the dealer upfront so the quote reflects the car's actual condition.

Paperwork and Legal

You do not legally need the V5C to scrap your car, but you should complete the yellow keeper section and send it to the DVLA to confirm you no longer own the vehicle. Without doing so, you remain the registered keeper on record until the dealer submits the Certificate of Destruction to the DVLA, which can take several weeks.

You can notify the DVLA online at gov.uk/scrap-your-vehicle, or by completing and posting the yellow keeper section of your V5C logbook to Swansea. The authorised treatment facility should also notify the DVLA electronically once the car is processed, but acting yourself ensures you are removed as keeper without delay.

A Certificate of Destruction (CoD) is the legal document issued by an Authorised Treatment Facility under the End of Life Vehicles (ELV) Regulations 2003. It confirms that the vehicle has been depolluted and scrapped at a licensed facility. You should receive a copy within seven days of your car being processed, and it serves as proof that you are no longer liable for the vehicle.

Scrapping a car you do not own without the registered keeper's consent is illegal. If you are scrapping a car on behalf of a family member or a deceased person's estate, the dealer will require proof of authority such as a letter of administration or written consent from the registered keeper. Always check with the dealer before proceeding.

If there is a finance agreement such as HP or PCP still outstanding, the finance company legally owns the car and you cannot scrap it without settling the debt or obtaining their written consent first. Scrapping a car with outstanding finance is fraud. Contact your finance provider before making any arrangements with a scrap dealer.

Payment

Payment must be made by bank transfer (BACS or Faster Payments) under the Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013. The dealer will ask for your bank account details at the point of collection and payment typically clears the same day or the next working day. Never accept a cheque from a dealer you do not know, as these can be cancelled or bounce.

No. It has been illegal to pay cash for scrap metal, including scrap cars, since the Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013 came into force. Any dealer offering cash in hand is operating outside the law and you should avoid them. Payment must be traceable by design, which is why bank transfer is the only permitted method.

Most authorised dealers process payment on the day of collection or the following working day. If you have not received payment within two working days of collection, contact the dealer directly using the details they provided. The Environment

Under the End of Life Vehicles (ELV) Regulations 2003, an Authorised Treatment Facility must first depollute the car, removing fluids, the battery and any hazardous materials safely. The remaining shell is then crushed and shredded, with metals separated and sent to steelmakers and smelters for recycling into new products.

By law, at least 95% of a car's weight must be recovered and reused or recycled under the ELV Regulations 2003, with at least 85% reused or recycled rather than used for energy recovery. The steel, aluminium, copper and plastics from your car will go back into manufacturing new products, making scrapping one of the most environmentally sound ways to dispose of a vehicle.

An Authorised Treatment Facility (ATF) is a site licensed by the Environment Agency (or SEPA in Scotland) to legally process end of life vehicles under the ELV Regulations 2003. Only ATFs can issue a Certificate of Destruction. All dealers on the ScrapCarCheck platform are licensed ATFs or work directly with one, so you can be confident your car is disposed of legally and responsibly.
