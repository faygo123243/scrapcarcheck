Can You Scrap a Car Without Keys?

Yes. Missing keys do not stop you scrapping a car. Here is how it affects the price and what you need instead.

Losing the keys to a car you want to scrap is more common than you might think. A car that has been sitting on the drive for months, bought from a private seller with one key, or simply lost over time, can still be collected and scrapped. There are a few things you should know first.

Yes, you can scrap a car without keys

Licensed Authorised Treatment Facilities (ATFs) accept keyless vehicles regularly. The car is going to be depolluted and shredded, not resold, so the ability to start it is not a requirement for acceptance. ATF drivers can winch or lift a car onto a transporter without needing to unlock or start it.

What the ATF does require is proof that you are the registered keeper or owner. The V5C logbook is the primary document for this. Your identity will also be verified in line with the Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013.

How missing keys affect the scrap price

Missing keys typically reduce the offer by £20 to £50 compared to an identical car with a full key set. The reason is practical: some ATFs or their buyers prefer cars with keys so they can test components or move the car under its own power within the facility. An ATF that cannot move the car at all and must winch it at every point factors in that additional handling cost.

The price reduction is not dramatic relative to the overall scrap value. For most cars, the difference is unlikely to exceed £50. If you have been quoted less than expected, mention it to other ATFs — some will quote the same price with or without keys.

Worth checking: Before assuming the keys are gone for good, check with your previous insurer, recovery company, or the last garage to service the car. Spare keys sometimes turn up in unexpected places and recovering them can increase your offer.

What ATFs need instead of keys

A licensed ATF will still want to verify ownership. The documents that matter are:

  • V5C logbook — This is the most important document. It shows you as the registered keeper and you will need to sign the V5C/3 section on collection day.
  • Photo ID — Driving licence or passport. Required by law regardless of whether you have keys.
  • Proof of address — Some ATFs ask for this (a utility bill or bank statement). Not universally required but worth having.

If you have the V5C and valid ID, the absence of keys should not prevent the collection from going ahead.

What if you also have no V5C?

Scrapping a car with neither keys nor a V5C is harder but not impossible. ATFs have a legal duty to satisfy themselves about ownership, and the V5C is the easiest way to do this. Without it, they may ask for additional documents — a purchase receipt if you bought the car, a DVLA reference letter, or a court document if ownership was transferred in unusual circumstances.

If you are the registered keeper but cannot find the V5C, apply for a duplicate from the DVLA at gov.uk (£25 online). This takes around five working days. Many people find it is worth waiting for the duplicate rather than trying to negotiate a keyless, logbook-free collection, which can be problematic.

If you did not buy the car through normal channels (for example you inherited it, found it abandoned on your land, or bought it at an auction without complete paperwork), speak to the ATF in advance. They will tell you what documentation they need before arranging collection.

Can you scrap a car that was stolen and recovered without keys?

Yes, provided you can show the police or insurer reference for the recovery and your own V5C. The ATF will note the circumstances. This is a common scenario, particularly where thieves have stripped the car or the ignition has been damaged. Let the ATF know the background when you get a quote.

Tips to maximise the offer without keys

  • Compare quotes from multiple ATFs — some are less affected by missing keys than others
  • Ensure all other components are present: wheels, battery, catalytic converter (these matter more than keys)
  • Have your V5C and ID ready to keep the collection moving smoothly
  • Be upfront about the missing keys when you get the quote — this avoids any revision on the day

Frequently asked questions

Will the ATF driver refuse to collect if there are no keys?

A legitimate ATF will not refuse collection solely because keys are missing, provided you have the V5C and valid ID. If a driver refuses or uses missing keys as a reason to heavily reduce the offer well below what was quoted, you can decline and re-quote elsewhere.

Do I need to declare that there are no keys when I get a quote?

Yes. Always be accurate when describing the car's condition at quote time. Mentioning missing keys upfront means the price offered is the price paid on the day. If you omit it and the driver finds out on collection, they may revise the offer downward, which is a weaker position to negotiate from.

Can the ATF get a replacement key and subtract the cost?

Some ATFs will arrange a replacement key and deduct the cost from your payment. This is more common where the car has resale value (which is less relevant for a true scrap vehicle). For a genuine end-of-life car, the ATF is unlikely to bother with a replacement key at all.

What about cars with immobilisers and no key?

If the car cannot be started due to an immobiliser and there is no key to override it, it is treated as a non-runner. This may affect the offer slightly but does not prevent collection. ATFs handle immobilised vehicles regularly.
