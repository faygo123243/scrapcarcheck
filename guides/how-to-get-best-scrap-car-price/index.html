How to Get the Best Scrap Car Price

Seven things that actually work — from comparison and timing to what to leave on the car.

The difference between the worst and best scrap offers for the same car can easily be £50 to £100. Most of that gap comes down to a few simple steps that most people skip. Here is what to do before you hand over the keys.

1

Compare multiple ATFs

Never accept the first offer. Prices vary by up to 30% between licensed ATFs for identical vehicles.

2

Keep the catalytic converter

The cat contains precious metals. Removing it before scrapping almost always costs you money overall.

3

Time it right

Spring and autumn tend to see higher steel prices. Avoid December and January if you can wait.

4

Be accurate about condition

Honest upfront — missing parts, damage, mileage. Surprises on collection day can reduce your final offer.

5

Leave all four wheels on

Alloy wheels may look sellable, but removing them typically reduces the scrap offer by more than they fetch.

6

Have the V5C ready

ATFs price higher for cars with paperwork. It confirms ownership and makes the transaction faster.

1. Always compare — do not call just one scrap yard

This is the single biggest factor. Scrap yards set their own prices based on local demand, their current capacity and their relationship with steel mills. A yard that is full of Fords this week may offer less than one that is short of Fords. Using a comparison service puts multiple offers in front of you instantly so you are not leaving money on the table by calling just one.

How much can comparing save you? On a typical family car, the gap between the lowest and highest offer from licensed ATFs is usually £30 to £80. On a heavier vehicle like an estate, SUV or van, it can be £100 or more.

2. Leave the catalytic converter intact

The catalytic converter on a petrol car built after 2000 contains platinum, palladium and rhodium — three precious metals that are worth real money. ATFs have specialist equipment to extract and sell these metals and factor the converter value into their offer. If you remove the cat before scrapping, the ATF simply deducts the value they would have recovered. You almost never come out ahead by stripping it yourself.

The exception: if you have a contact who buys cats directly and you know the specific metal content, you may be able to negotiate. For most people, this is not worth the time or the risk of getting an inaccurate value.

3. Keep all four wheels and the battery

Every component you remove that the ATF would have recovered reduces your offer. Alloy wheels may look like easy eBay money, but a standard set from a 2012 Ford Focus might fetch £80 to £120 online after listing fees, postage or buyer collection hassle. The ATF deducts a similar amount when wheels are missing. It is rarely worth it unless the wheels are genuinely sought after for that specific model.

The 12V battery is the same story. A used car battery sells for £20 to £40, but the ATF deducts that value from the offer if it is missing. Leave it in.

4. Time it for spring or autumn

Global steel prices follow a loose seasonal cycle driven by construction activity. Spring (March to May) and autumn (September to October) tend to see higher prices as mills restock and construction activity picks up. December and January are typically the weakest months. If your car is on a SORN and storage is not a problem, waiting for a better part of the cycle can add £20 to £50 to the offer.

5. Be accurate about condition from the start

Give the ATF accurate information about the car's condition when you get a quote. If you describe a car as complete and running when it is actually a non-runner with a missing battery, the driver will spot it on collection and the offer will be revised downward. Getting a revised offer at the kerbside is a weak negotiating position. Accuracy upfront means the agreed price holds.

6. Have your V5C (logbook) ready

Scrap dealers can legally accept a car without a V5C, but they prefer to have it. It confirms you are the legal owner and makes the documentation cleaner for both parties. Some ATFs pay slightly better for cars with paperwork. If you have lost the V5C, apply for a replacement from the DVLA before scrapping — it costs £25 and can add more than that to your offer.

7. Check the fuel in the tank

This sounds minor but a car with a near-empty tank versus half a tank does not meaningfully change the scrap offer. The ATF drains all fluids anyway. Do not fill the car up before scrapping — that money is gone. Similarly, do not spend money on a valet or any cosmetic work. Scrap price is weight-based, not presentation-based.

Things that do not help: Cleaning the car, replacing tyres, topping up fluids, repairing minor bodywork or paying for an MOT. None of these change the scrap offer. Save that money.

Watch out for unlicensed scrap dealers

Unlicensed scrap dealers sometimes offer slightly higher headline prices but cannot legally pay you by bank transfer or issue a Certificate of Destruction. This means you remain on record as the registered keeper after the car has left your possession — which creates liability risk. Always use a licensed ATF. Frequently asked questions

Will a scrap dealer negotiate on price?

The headline offer from a licensed ATF is generally firm because it is based on the current steel price and the vehicle's weight and condition. The best way to negotiate is to have a competing offer from another ATF — that is a genuine lever. Asking for more without evidence rarely works.

Does my car's colour affect the scrap price?

No. Scrap price is weight and metal content based. Colour, trim level and optional extras do not affect it at all.

Does a full service history increase scrap value?

Not directly. A full service history may make a car easier to sell privately, but it adds nothing to a scrap offer. The only thing that changes a scrap offer is the weight of the car and the current price of steel.
