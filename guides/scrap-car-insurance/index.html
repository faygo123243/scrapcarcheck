Scrap Car Insurance — What to Do With It

Cancel at the right time, protect your no-claims discount and get back any unused premium.

When you scrap your car, you will have an active insurance policy that needs cancelling — or at least notifying. Getting this wrong can mean losing money on unused premium, forfeiting your no-claims discount, or even creating a gap in your cover history. Here is exactly what to do.

When should you cancel your insurance?

The right time to cancel is the day the car is collected by the ATF and you have received your Certificate of Destruction (CoD) or at least a written confirmation that the car has been accepted. Do not cancel before collection — you need to be insured while the car is still in your name and sitting on a public road.

Do not cancel too early. If you cancel before the car is collected and it is involved in an accident or causes damage while being transported to the ATF, you could be personally liable. Keep cover active until the CoD confirms the car is no longer yours.

Can you get a refund on unused insurance?

Yes, in most cases. If you cancel mid-policy, your insurer should refund the unused portion of your premium minus a cancellation fee. Cancellation fees vary by insurer — typically between £25 and £75. Some policies are non-refundable if you are close to the renewal date.

Call your insurer as soon as the car is scrapped and provide your Certificate of Destruction number. They may ask for proof the car has been disposed of, and the CoD serves as exactly that.

What happens to your no-claims discount?

Your no-claims discount (NCD) is attached to you as a driver, not to the specific vehicle. When you cancel a policy because you have scrapped the car, your insurer will issue a letter or certificate showing the number of years of no-claims you have accrued. Keep this document — you will need it when you take out a new policy on another car.

Tip: Ask your insurer to issue a no-claims confirmation letter at the same time you notify them of the cancellation. Some do this automatically; others need to be asked.

Step by step: what to do with your insurance when scrapping

  1. Book your scrap collection and confirm the date with the ATF. Keep your insurance active until this date.
  2. Hand over the car on collection day. Make sure the ATF gives you a receipt or confirms the transaction in writing.
  3. Notify the DVLA using the V5C logbook or the online service. The ATF may do this on your behalf — confirm which applies.
  4. Wait for your Certificate of Destruction. This arrives by post within seven days from a licensed ATF. You can also check its status at gov.uk.
  5. Contact your insurer with the CoD number and cancel the policy. Request a refund and a no-claims confirmation letter.
  6. Check for a tax refund. If your vehicle excise duty (road tax) has months remaining, you will receive an automatic refund from the DVLA. See our guide to the scrap car tax refund for details.

What if my car is on a SORN?

If the car is declared SORN (Statutory Off Road Notification), you are legally permitted to keep it uninsured while it is off the road. However, once it is on a public road — even briefly during collection — it must be insured or the ATF must carry it on their trade plates. Reputable ATFs collect on trade plates so your own insurance does not need to cover the movement.

Do I need to tell my insurer before I scrap?

You do not need to ask permission, but most policies require you to notify your insurer of any material change — including disposing of the vehicle. It is good practice to tell them a few days before collection so there are no queries about the end date of cover.

What if I am still making monthly payments on my policy?

Monthly payment insurance policies are typically an annual policy split into monthly instalments. If you cancel early, the insurer may require you to pay the remaining balance as a lump sum or deduct it from any refund owed. Check the terms of your policy before cancelling to avoid a surprise bill.

Frequently asked questions

Will cancelling my insurance affect my credit score?

Cancelling a car insurance policy does not directly affect your credit score. Some insurers carry out a soft credit check when you take out a new policy, but cancellation itself has no credit impact.

Can I transfer my insurance to a new car?

Yes. Rather than cancelling, you may be able to transfer your existing policy to a new vehicle. Call your insurer and ask about a vehicle substitution. This can save you the cancellation fee and means your cover continues without a gap.

What if I cannot find my Certificate of Destruction?

You can check the status of a scrapped vehicle on the DVLA's vehicle enquiry service at gov.uk using the registration number. A licensed ATF is legally required to issue the CoD within seven days — if you have not received one, contact the ATF directly.
