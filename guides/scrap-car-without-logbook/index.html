Can I Scrap a Car Without a V5C Logbook?

Yes — here's exactly how to do it

Losing your V5C log book is frustrating, but it doesn't mean you can't scrap your car. Most authorised scrap dealers will still accept your vehicle — they just need a bit more paperwork from you. Here's everything you need to know.

Short answer: Yes, you can scrap a car without a V5C. You'll need to prove ownership another way and notify the DVLA separately using form V62.

Why dealers ask for the V5C

The V5C (Vehicle Registration Certificate) proves you're the registered keeper of the vehicle. Authorised Treatment Facilities (ATFs) — the only legal places to scrap a car — are required by law to verify ownership before accepting a vehicle. This helps prevent stolen vehicles entering the scrap chain.

However, the V5C isn't the only way to prove you own the car.

What to do if you've lost your V5C

Your first step should be applying for a replacement V5C from the DVLA. It costs £25 and takes around a week to arrive. You can apply online at gov.uk, by phone, or by post using form V62.

If you need to scrap the car before the replacement arrives, many dealers will accept:

  • A V5C/3 (the yellow "new keeper" slip, if you recently bought the car)
  • Proof of purchase (receipt, auction document)
  • V62 application receipt (shows you've applied for a replacement)
  • A statutory declaration (signed statement of ownership)

Always call the dealer in advance — acceptance of alternatives varies by ATF.

Notifying the DVLA without a V5C

Normally when you scrap a car, the ATF sends off the V5C section 9 to the DVLA. Without a V5C, you must notify the DVLA yourself using form V62.

Once the car is scrapped, the ATF will give you a Certificate of Destruction (CoD). Keep this safe — it's your proof that the vehicle has been legally destroyed and you're no longer liable for it.

What if someone else is still shown as the keeper?

If you bought the car privately and the previous keeper never updated the V5C, you'll need to show your proof of purchase. Some ATFs may require you to sort the registration with the DVLA first, especially for higher-value vehicles. This prevents fraudulent scrapping.

Can a scrap dealer be fined for not checking?

Yes. ATFs face significant penalties for accepting vehicles without proper ownership verification. This is why reputable dealers are thorough — don't take it personally if they ask for extra documentation.
