Scrap vs Part Exchange: Which Gets You More Money?

The honest comparison — no spin

When it's time to move on from your old car, you have two main options: scrap it for cash, or part-exchange it at a dealership against a new or used car. Which one puts more money in your pocket depends heavily on your car's condition and age. Here's the straight comparison.

♻ Scrapping

  • Price based on weight (metal value)
  • Works for any condition, even non-runners
  • Cash paid to your bank account
  • Free collection from your door
  • Best for high-mileage, damaged or very old cars
  • No need to negotiate

🚗 Part Exchange

  • Price based on resale market value
  • Requires a running, presentable car
  • Discount off new car — not cash
  • You drive the car to the dealer
  • Best for cars under 10 years with reasonable mileage
  • Room to negotiate (but dealers often lowball)

When scrapping beats part exchange

For most cars over 10 years old, high-mileage (over 100,000 miles), or with significant mechanical issues or bodywork damage, scrapping will almost certainly pay more than part exchange.

Dealers applying a part-exchange will factor in reconditioning costs — respray, service, MOT — before pricing your car. An older car often gets offered £200–£500 as a "goodwill" part-exchange that's really just to get you to sign a deal. A scrap dealer will pay you on metal weight alone, which for a full-size family car typically comes to £250–£400 regardless of whether it drives.

When part exchange beats scrapping

A car under 8 years old, with full service history, under 80,000 miles, and in clean condition can command real part-exchange value. A five-year-old mid-range family car in good condition might fetch £4,000–£8,000 on part exchange, versus £300–£450 for scrap. For these cars, part exchange — or better yet, private sale — wins easily.

The private sale option

If your car has any meaningful resale value, selling it privately on Auto Trader or eBay Motors will almost always beat both part exchange and scrap. The trade-off is time and effort: photography, listing, viewings, test drives, and price negotiation. For a car worth over £1,000, it's usually worth it.

The "negotiating chip" myth

Some dealers present part exchange as a discount rather than a cash figure — making it feel more generous than it is. Always ask: "What would you pay me cash for this car, if I wasn't buying from you?" That reveals the true value they're placing on your old car, and gives you a basis for negotiation or walking away.

Our verdict: If your car is old, high-mileage, damaged or not running — scrap it and don't look back. If it's relatively modern and in good shape, get at least three part-exchange quotes and compare them to a private sale price. The difference can be thousands of pounds.
