Does a Scrap Car Need an MOT?

No. Here is why, and what you actually need in place before your car is collected.

One of the most common misconceptions about scrapping a car is that it needs a valid MOT. It does not. You can scrap a car with an expired MOT, a failed MOT, or no MOT history at all — and the process is no different from scrapping a car that has one.

The short answer: No MOT is required to scrap a car in the UK. Dealers collect on a flatbed truck and do not drive the vehicle on the road, so roadworthiness is not relevant.

Why no MOT is needed

The MOT test exists to confirm that a vehicle is safe to drive on public roads. When you scrap a car, it is collected on a flatbed truck or trailer and taken directly to an Authorised Treatment Facility (ATF) for processing. It never drives under its own power on a public road, so the MOT requirement simply does not apply.

The same logic applies to road tax. You do not need a current tax disc to scrap your car. If you have unexpired road tax, the DVLA will refund any remaining full months automatically once they are notified that the car has been scrapped.

What you do need

While an MOT is not required, a few other things are worth having in order:

  • Proof of ownership. You should be the registered keeper, or have documentation showing you own the vehicle. Dealers are legally required to verify ownership before accepting a car.
  • Your V5C logbook. This is not strictly required but speeds up the process considerably. If you do not have it, see our guide: Scrapping a car without a V5C logbook.
  • Your bank details. Under the Scrap Metal Dealers Act 2013, payment must be made by bank transfer. Have your sort code and account number ready.

What about a failed MOT?

A failed MOT does not prevent you from scrapping a car. Many people choose to scrap rather than repair a car that has failed its MOT, particularly when the repair cost would exceed the car's value. Dealers will collect it in the same way regardless of why the MOT failed.

You do not need to tell the dealer that the MOT has failed — it is simply not relevant to the transaction. What matters is the car's weight, condition and the metal prices on the day of collection.

Does an MOT affect the scrap price?

Not directly. Scrap price is based primarily on the weight of the car and current metal prices, not its roadworthiness. A car with a valid MOT and one without will generally receive the same quote for the same make, model, weight and condition.

That said, a car with a recent MOT may still be worth more if it is in good enough condition to sell privately or part-exchange rather than scrap. If your car has a current MOT and runs well, it may be worth exploring alternatives to scrapping before committing. See our guide: Scrap vs part exchange: which gets you more?

Can I drive a car with no MOT to a scrap yard?

No. Driving a vehicle without a valid MOT on a public road is illegal, and doing so could result in a fine and penalty points. If your car has an expired or no MOT, the correct approach is to have the dealer collect it on a flatbed, which is standard practice and included in the service at no extra charge with most authorised dealers.

Do not drive a car with an expired MOT to a scrap yard. It is illegal and unnecessary. Authorised dealers collect using a flatbed truck as standard.

Summary

You do not need an MOT to scrap your car. The MOT is irrelevant to the scrapping process because the car is collected by flatbed, not driven. What you do need is proof of ownership, ideally your V5C, and your bank details for payment. Everything else is handled by the dealer.
