How to Scrap a Non-Runner Car

It doesn't move — that's fine. Here's what you need to know

Whether your car won't start, has a blown engine, or has been sitting on your drive for years, you can absolutely scrap it. In fact, scrapping is often the best option for a non-runner. Here's the complete picture.

Key point: Most ATF scrap dealers offer free collection for non-runners using a flatbed or tow truck. You don't pay anything — the dealer deducts collection costs from your quote, and you still receive payment by bank transfer.

What counts as a non-runner?

A non-runner is any vehicle that can't be driven under its own power to the scrap yard. This includes:

  • Cars with dead batteries or no engine
  • Vehicles with severe gearbox or clutch failure
  • Flood-damaged cars
  • Cars that have been in accidents and aren't roadworthy
  • Vehicles that have simply seized up through lack of use
  • SORN'd cars on private land

How does collection work for a non-runner?

Reputable scrap dealers have specialist recovery vehicles — flatbed trucks and tow trucks — specifically for collecting non-runners. The process is:

  • Get a quote online or by phone (you'll be asked about the car's condition)
  • Book a collection date — most dealers offer next-day or same-week slots
  • The recovery truck arrives and loads your car
  • You hand over your V5C or provide alternative ID proof
  • Payment is made to your bank account (usually same day)

You'll need to make sure the car is accessible — on your driveway or in a car park with enough clearance for the flatbed to load. Cars in garages, underground car parks, or with very restricted access may incur additional charges.

How much will I get for a non-runner?

Non-runners are priced on scrap metal value — essentially the weight of the vehicle multiplied by the current steel price. A typical non-runner quote for an average family car is £150–£350, depending on:

  • Vehicle weight (heavier cars = more steel = more money)
  • Whether major components are still present (engine, gearbox, catalytic converter)
  • Current scrap metal prices
  • Distance from the nearest ATF

If your car is genuinely non-running but otherwise complete (all its parts still present), the deduction from a running car's price is usually modest — perhaps 10–20%.

Can I scrap a car on SORN?

Yes. A SORN (Statutory Off Road Notification) simply means the vehicle isn't taxed or insured for road use. Scrapping a SORN'd car is perfectly legal, provided the dealer collects from private land (not a public road) and you have proper ownership documentation.

Once scrapped, DVLA will automatically cancel the SORN when they receive the Certificate of Destruction.

What if the car has been off the road for years?

Long-dormant vehicles can still be scrapped. However, if the car has been abandoned on land that isn't yours, or is missing significant components, you may need to clarify ownership before a dealer will collect.

For vehicles over 10 years old with no recent MOT history, expect scrap-only pricing with no part-value uplift.
